Structural Geology

The study of how rocks deform — folded, fractured, tilted, and faulted by the stresses of tectonics. This section holds reference material and working tools for reading orientation data, folds, and faults in the field or from a map.

Core concepts

A quick refresher on the vocabulary the tools below build on.

Strike & Dip

Strike is the compass bearing of a horizontal line on a planar surface; dip is the angle that surface plunges below horizontal, measured perpendicular to strike.

Folds

Ductile bending of layered rock. Anticlines arch upward with the oldest beds in the core; synclines sag downward with the youngest beds in the core.

Reference

Anatomy of a fold

  • HingeLine of maximum curvature along the fold; where the axial plane intersects the folded surface.
  • LimbThe flank of a fold between two hinges, typically the most planar part.
  • Axial planeThe surface that divides a fold as symmetrically as possible, connecting hinge lines through the stack of folded layers.
  • VergenceThe direction a fold appears to lean or verge, used to infer the location of a larger structure like a fold nose or thrust front.
  • Inflection pointWhere the folded surface crosses its own regional trend line, marking the boundary between one limb and the next.
